QUERY: Explain how decoupled weight decay in AdamW prevents exponential weight explosion compared to standard L2 regularization.
FILTER: None

============================================================
SIMILARITY RETRIEVER ANSWER:
============================================================
Decoupled weight decay in AdamW prevents exponential weight explosion compared to standard L2 regularization by separating the weight decay term from the gradient updates, ensuring that the regularization is applied independently of the gradient moving averages.

In standard Adam, L2 regularization is incorporated directly into the gradient computation, which means that the weight decay term is added to the gradient before it is used to update the parameters. This can lead to an exponential amplification of the weights during training, especially when the gradients are large or noisy, because the regularization is applied in a way that interacts with the gradient dynamics and can exacerbate instability.

In contrast, AdamW decouples the weight decay factor $\lambda$ from the gradient moving averages. The update rule in AdamW is:
$$
\theta_t = \theta_{t-1} - \eta \left( \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} + \lambda \theta_{t-1} \right)
$$
Here, the term $\lambda \theta_{t-1}$ is applied directly to the parameter value, independent of the gradient computation. This decoupling ensures that the regularization effect is consistent and does not interact with the gradient scaling, thereby preventing exponential weight explosion during training.

This separation allows for more stable and effective optimization, particularly in deep neural networks and transformer backbones, where large parameter spaces and high-dimensional gradients are common.
