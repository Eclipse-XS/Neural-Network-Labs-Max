QUERY: Explain how decoupled weight decay in AdamW prevents exponential weight explosion compared to standard L2 regularization.
FILTER: None

============================================================
Decoupled weight decay in AdamW prevents exponential weight explosion compared to standard L2 regularization by decoupling the weight decay term from the gradient updates, ensuring that the regularization is applied independently of the gradient moving averages.

In standard Adam, L2 regularization is incorporated directly into the gradient computation, which means that the weight decay term is added to the gradient before it is used to update the parameters. This can lead to an exponential amplification of the weights during training because the gradient updates are simultaneously influenced by both the gradient dynamics and the regularization term, especially when the gradients are large or noisy. As a result, the regularization effect is not consistently applied and may be overwhelmed by large gradient updates, leading to instability and potential explosion of weights.

In contrast, AdamW decouples the weight decay factor $\lambda$ from the gradient moving averages. The update rule in AdamW is:
$$
\theta_t = \theta_{t-1} - \eta \left( \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} + \lambda \theta_{t-1} \right)
$$
Here, the weight decay term $\lambda \theta_{t-1}$ is applied directly to the parameter value $\theta_{t-1}$, independent of the gradient computation. This ensures that the regularization is consistently applied to the parameter values throughout training, without being modulated by the gradient magnitude or the adaptive learning rate. As a result, the regularization effect is stable and does not interact with the gradient dynamics in a way that could amplify weights.

This decoupling prevents exponential weight explosion because the weight decay term acts as a consistent stabilizing force on the parameter values, regardless of the magnitude of the gradients. In contrast, standard L2 regularization, when embedded in the gradient update, can interact with large gradients and lead to unstable updates that may cause weights to grow exponentially.
