QUERY: Explain how decoupled weight decay in AdamW prevents exponential weight explosion compared to standard L2 regularization.
FILTER: None

============================================================
SIMILARITY RETRIEVER ANSWER:
============================================================
Decoupled weight decay in AdamW prevents exponential weight explosion compared to standard L2 regularization by decoupling the weight decay term from the gradient updates, ensuring that the regularization is applied independently of the gradient moving averages.

In standard Adam, L2 regularization is incorporated directly into the gradient computation, which means that the weight decay term is added to the gradient before it is used to update the parameters. This can lead to an exponential amplification of the weights during training because the decay term interacts with the gradient updates in a way that may exacerbate instability, especially when gradients are large or noisy.

In contrast, AdamW decouples the weight decay factor $\lambda$ from the gradient moving averages. The update rule in AdamW is:
$$
\theta_t = \theta_{t-1} - \eta \left( \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} + \lambda \theta_{t-1} \right)
$$
Here, the weight decay term $\lambda \theta_{t-1}$ is applied directly to the parameter value $\theta_{t-1}$, independent of the gradient computation. This separation ensures that the regularization effect is not modulated by the gradient dynamics, thus avoiding the exponential growth of weights that can occur when the regularization is embedded within the gradient update.

This decoupling prevents exponential weight explosion because the weight decay acts as a stabilizing force on the parameter values themselves, rather than being subject to the same amplification effects as the gradient updates. As a result, the model maintains better stability and generalization during training.
